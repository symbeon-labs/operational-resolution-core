# Research Principles

## 1. Preserve before resolving

A resolution process must not destroy the assertions that produced it.

## 2. Evidence is not the same as assertion

An assertion states something. Evidence supports an assertion. They should remain distinguishable.

## 3. Identity is not relation

An entity and a relationship involving that entity are different semantic objects.

## 4. Observation is not expectation

What should happen and what was observed must be representable independently.

## 5. Conflict is information

Contradictory assertions should remain visible when they cannot be legitimately reconciled.

## 6. Resolution is contextual

The operational result depends on the question, context and applicable rules.

## 7. Intelligence does not become fact automatically

Inference may produce an assertion, but an inference should retain its provenance and epistemic status.

## 8. Consequence requires traceability

The stronger the operational consequence, the stronger the requirement to preserve evidence and resolution provenance.

## 9. Determinism where possible, intelligence where necessary

Deterministic rules should handle well-defined conditions. Intelligence may assist where uncertainty or interpretation is unavoidable.

## 10. Falsification before implementation

The model must be tested against real cases and existing approaches before becoming a protocol or implementation.
